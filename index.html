Why local

National averages hide the story

AI is the same technology everywhere, but each economy absorbs it differently. A plan built on national numbers will be wrong for most places, so every report is built for your state, labor market and institution.

What people do

The local mix of jobs sets how much work shrinks. Office, retail and logistics economies feel it sooner than health, science and public-sector ones.

What work pays

Local wages set when AI pays first. The higher the cost of labor, the sooner AI is worth adopting.

Where people commute

Workers cross county and state lines. Plans have to follow the real labor market, not the map.

What local programs exist

Displaced workers need bridges close to home: local colleges, training providers and apprenticeships.

Questions

Frequently asked

How is this different from a labor-market dashboard?

Dashboards show past job postings. We forecast to 2035 at the occupation level, separate the work AI removes from the jobs demand adds, and tie both to your specific programs. The result is a decision for each program, not a chart to interpret.

Where do the numbers come from?

Federal public data: BLS occupational employment and wages, Census commuting flows, IPEDS completions, and O*NET task data, combined with a published scenario for AI adoption. The methods are documented in our open research, and every source is listed with the public data.

How certain are the forecasts?

They are estimates under one scenario, not predictions. Every report shows ranges, flags programs whose range crosses zero, and lists its limits. We recommend renewing each year as new data arrive; see how the estimates are updated.

Do you need our internal data?

No. A first report uses public data only. If you share program-level placement or noncredit enrollment, we can add it, and it stays confidential to you.

Can we publish the report?

Yes. You decide whether a report stays internal or is shared with your board, partners or the public.

Which places do you cover?

All 50 states and the District of Columbia, down to counties, commuting markets and individual colleges. Our sample reports use an illustrative state and county.

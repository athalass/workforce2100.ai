Sources we use

How AI changes work

  • O*NET 31.0 (U.S. Department of Labor): tasks, work context, skills
  • Eloundou, Manning, Mishkin & Rock (2024), Science: which tasks AI can do
  • Anthropic Economic Index, Claude.ai task data (January 2026): task success and time saved
  • Census Business Trends and Outlook Survey (November 2025 to October 2026): speed of AI adoption
  • Census Statistics of U.S. Businesses 2022: employment weights for adoption

Jobs, growth and where new jobs land

  • BLS Employment Projections 2025–35: growth, separations, entry education, and BLS's own AI adjustments
  • BLS National Employment Matrix 2025–35
  • BLS input-output tables (real final demand, 2025 and 2035)
  • BLS domestic employment requirements table (2025)
  • BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS), May 2025: employment, wages, staffing patterns
  • BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW) 2025: county employment by industry
  • Weldon Cooper Center national population projections (vintage 2026): school-age drivers

Places, commuting and moving

  • USDA Economic Research Service 2020 Commuting Zones
  • Census LODES 8 (2024): where workers live
  • Census county boundaries 2024: distances
  • Census American Community Survey 2020–24, table C24010: county check only

Colleges and programs

  • NCES IPEDS Completions 2023–24 and Institutional Characteristics
  • NCES/BLS CIP 2020–SOC 2018 crosswalk
  • Census Post-Secondary Employment Outcomes (PSEO): graduate outcomes, and the share of graduates working in the state
  • DOL Eligible Training Provider list (TrainingProviderResults.gov)
  • DOL list of apprenticeable occupations

Model and scripts

  • Thalassinidis (2026), Where Work Is Moving, on OSF (osf.io/ae95y)

Each state's workbook lists its own state sources.

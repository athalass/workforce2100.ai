State level

Sample State: five findings

About 700 occupations and 1.6 million jobs, 2025 to 2035. The state gains about 29,000 jobs on net, but about 93,000 workers are in occupations that shrink.

  1. Change is concentrated.

    Office and administrative support holds half of the jobs in declining occupations. Office, sales and transportation together hold three quarters. Secretaries, retail salespersons and customer service representatives lose the most jobs.

  2. Growth is in care, building and technology.

    Home health aides, nursing assistants, registered nurses, construction trades and computer occupations add the most jobs.

  3. Higher wages do not change where AI pays today.

    At today's AI cost, AI pays for about 47% of task-hours with state or U.S. wages alike. Wages matter only if AI costs $10–20 per hour of work. They do raise the stakes: about $21 billion a year in state wages is tied to the work hours AI removes.

  4. Most programs need new curriculum, not closure.

    Of 48,500 completions at 45 institutions, 22% are in programs to invest in, 77% in programs to reinvent and 1.6% in programs to review. In 85% of completions, AI can do half or more of graduates' task time.

  5. Pathways exist, but crowd onto a few occupations.

    For 74% of job losses, the best transfer route already has an in-state program, provider or apprenticeship. But the closest skill matches are small occupations; most displaced workers will need to move into care, trades and technology.

Your programs · example

The program map

Every credential a college awards, placed by how demand for its graduates changes to 2035 and how much of their work AI can do. One picture tells a board where to grow, what to redesign and what to rethink.

Invest

Demand for graduates grows 10% or more by 2035.

Grow enrollment and capacity. If the program sits above the AI line, redesign it as it grows.

Reinvent

Demand changes between −10% and +10%.

Keep the program, but change what it teaches: directing AI, checking its work, and the human skills AI cannot do.

Review

Demand falls more than 10%.

Re-aim toward growing occupations, merge or wind down, after checking local placement. Programs whose range reaches zero are flagged uncertain.

The AI line

AI can do half or more of graduates' task time.

Programs above the dashed line need new curriculum whatever their tier, including growing ones.

How to read the map. Left to right: demand change for graduates. Bottom to top: share of their task time AI can do. Circle size: awards per year. Hover or tap a circle for details.

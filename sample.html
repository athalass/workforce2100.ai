Sample findings · Maryland to 2035

What our reports find, and how they read

Excerpts from our Maryland study: the state, how impact differs by place, one county, worker transition paths, and an illustrative college's programs. Your report follows the same structure for your state, region or college.

State level

Maryland: five findings

703 occupations and 2.72 million jobs, 2025 to 2035. Maryland gains about 48,000 jobs on net, but about 155,000 workers are in occupations that shrink.

  1. Change is concentrated.

    Office and administrative support holds half of the jobs in declining occupations. Office, sales and transportation together hold three quarters. Secretaries, retail salespersons and customer service representatives lose the most jobs.

  2. Growth is in care, building and technology.

    Home health aides, nursing assistants, registered nurses, construction trades and computer occupations add the most jobs.

  3. Higher wages do not change where AI pays today.

    At today's AI cost, AI pays for about 47% of task-hours with Maryland or U.S. wages alike. Wages matter only if AI costs $10–20 per hour of work. They do raise the stakes: about $35 billion a year in Maryland wages is tied to the work hours AI removes.

  4. Most programs need new curriculum, not closure.

    Of 80,900 completions at 75 institutions, 22% are in programs to invest in, 77% in programs to reinvent and 1.6% in programs to review. In 85% of completions, AI can do half or more of graduates' task time.

  5. Pathways exist, but crowd onto a few occupations.

    For 74% of job losses, the best transfer route already has a Maryland program, provider or apprenticeship. But the closest skill matches are small occupations; most displaced workers will need to move into care, trades and technology.

Geography

AI does not hit every place the same way

The same technology lands differently depending on what a place's jobs are, what they pay, and where its residents commute. That is why we report by state, county and labor market, not just nationally.

What people do

Industry mix sets how much work shrinks.

Counties with more office, retail and transportation jobs have more workers in declining occupations. Places built on federal, defense, science and health work have fewer.

What work pays

Wages set when AI pays first.

At today's AI cost, AI pays for the same tasks everywhere. If AI costs rise to $10–20 per hour of work, it pays first where labor costs most: in Maryland, the Washington suburbs, Lexington Park and the Baltimore region, and later the Eastern Shore and Western Maryland.

Where people commute

Residents and jobs are not the same thing.

39% of Prince George's residents work outside Maryland. A plan for residents has to follow the labor market they actually work in.

Worker transitions

From a shrinking job to a growing one

For every shrinking occupation we find the growing occupations whose skills fit best, how far the move is, what it pays, and which local programs, training providers and apprenticeships can carry workers across.

Direct

Most skills carry over; same or higher pay; under 6 months of training.

A short credential or apprenticeship is enough.

Winding

About half the skills carry over; about 90% of the pay; under 2 years.

A certificate or associate degree, ideally part-time and stackable.

Unpaved

Everything else: a longer climb.

Often a degree, but it can lead to much higher pay.

The capacity check

A good match only helps if there are jobs to fill.

We compare the workers headed to each destination with its new jobs, and steer plans toward occupations big enough to absorb them.

Institution level · example

The program map

Every credential a college awards, placed by how demand for its graduates changes to 2035 and how much of their work AI can do. One picture tells a board where to grow, what to redesign and what to rethink.

Invest

Demand for graduates grows 10% or more by 2035.

Grow enrollment and capacity. If the program sits above the AI line, redesign it as it grows.

Reinvent

Demand changes between −10% and +10%.

Keep the program, but change what it teaches: directing AI, checking its work, and the human skills AI cannot do.

Review

Demand falls more than 10%.

Re-aim toward growing occupations, merge or wind down, after checking local placement. Programs whose range reaches zero are flagged uncertain.

The AI line

AI can do half or more of graduates' task time.

Programs above the dashed line need new curriculum whatever their tier, including growing ones.

How to read the map. Left to right: demand change for graduates. Bottom to top: share of their task time AI can do. Circle size: awards per year. Hover or tap a circle for details.
